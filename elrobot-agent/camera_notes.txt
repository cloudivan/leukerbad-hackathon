Camera index 0 = TOP-DOWN overview, fixed above the table, looking straight down.
  The robot base is at the top centre of the picture and the arm points DOWN the picture.
  Picture DOWN  = +X (forward, away from the base).
  Picture RIGHT = +Y (the robot's left).  Picture LEFT = -Y.
  Use it for x and y: where the object is relative to the base and to the gripper. It shows no height.
  The laptop at the right edge of this picture is the side camera (index 2).

Camera index 1 = WRIST camera, mounted on the arm behind the jaws, moves with the gripper.
  It looks down and forward. The two dark shapes at the bottom edge are the jaws.
  With yaw 0: picture UP = +X (forward), picture LEFT = +Y.
  WARNING, parallax: an object that appears just above or between the jaws in this picture is
  really several centimetres IN FRONT of the jaw tips. Never decide from this camera alone that
  the jaws are over the object.

Camera index 2 = SIDE view from the laptop, fixed at table height on the robot's left (+Y side).
  The robot base is on the right of the picture and the arm reaches to the LEFT.
  Picture LEFT = +X (forward).  Picture UP = +Z (height).  It shows no left/right (y).
  Use it for depth: how far in front of the jaw tips the object is, and how high the tips are
  above the table. The jaws are correctly placed in x when the jaw tips are directly above the
  object in this picture.

Procedure that works: find the object's x and y in camera 0, move above it, then confirm in
camera 2 that the jaw tips are directly above it before going down. If camera 2 shows the object
further left than the tips, move +x; further right, move -x.
